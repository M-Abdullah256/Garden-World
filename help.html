Frequently Asked Questions

When selecting plants, consider factors like climate, sunlight, soil type, and space availability . Research whether your chosen plants thrive in full sun, partial shade, or indoors . Also, check their water and maintenance requirements.

A mix of compost, garden soil, and sand is great for vegetables.

  • Loamy soil – Ideal for most vegetables and flowers
  • Sandy soil – Good for succulents and cacti (drains quickly)
  • Clay soil – Retains moisture but needs improvement with compost
  • Peat soil – Suitable for moisture-loving plants like ferns

Watering frequency depends on plant type, weather, and soil conditions. General guideline:

  • Indoor plants: Once a week (adjust based on humidity)
  • Outdoor plants: 2-3 times a week(more in summer, less in winter)
  • Succulents & cacti: Once every 2-3 weeks

Always check the soil moisture before watering to avoid overwatering.

Basic gardening tools include:

  • Hand trowel & garden fork (for digging and planting)
  • Pruning shears (for trimming and cutting)
  • Watering can or hose (for irrigation)
  • Gloves & kneeling pad (for protection and comfort)

To control pests naturally:

  • Use companion planting (e.g., marigolds repel aphids)
  • Spray a homemade neem oil solution on affected plants
  • Introduce beneficial insectslike ladybugs to eat harmful pests
  • Keep your garden clean and free of weedsto reduce pest hiding spots

There are two main types of fertilizers:

  • Organic fertilizers Compost, manure, fish emulsion, bone meal
  • Synthetic fertilizersNPK-based (Nitrogen-Phosphorus-Potassium) blends for specific plant needs

Use slow-release fertilizers for continuous nourishment and avoid over-fertilizing, as it can damage plant roots.

For container gardening:

  • Choose pots with drainage holes
  • Use high-quality potting mix
  • Water regularly and place in sufficient sunlight (6-8 hours daily)
  • Grow easy vegetables like tomatoes, lettuce, peppers, and herbs

For container gardening:

  • Mix green waste (vegetable scraps, fruit peels, grass clippings) with brown waste (dry leaves, cardboard, wood chips)
  • Keep the pile moist but not soggy
  • Turn the compost every few weeks for aeration
  • In 2-3 months, you'll have nutrient-rich compost for your plants!
